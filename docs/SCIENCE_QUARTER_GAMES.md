## Shared interface

- The existing parchment header supplies the activity number, title, subtitle, challenge counter, and score.
- **How to Play:** top right, logical position `(1135, 37)`. Three illustrated steps and a concise scientist tip.
- **Settings:** top right, `(1209, 37)`. Music and sound-effect preferences preserve the experiment state.
- **Back:** bottom left, `(46, 678)`. Returns to the lesson map at the previous scroll position.
- A consistent bottom strip summarizes the activity. No pause button or timer appears in these experiments.
- Experiment controls occupy the left bench; observations, feedback, Reset/Retry, and Check/Next share the right panel.
- Reset clears the current trial. Retry replaces the current challenge's score rather than awarding it twice.

Each activity awards up to 25 points per challenge, with a small reduction for unsuccessful result checks and a minimum of 10 on successful completion. Every challenge can be retried. Completed lessons are marked on the map.

## Persistence and written work

Results are stored in browser `localStorage` under `lumify-science-q1-v1`. Existing completed challenges remain saved when a lesson is revisited. Starting a lesson opens a fresh practice session; this is not an automatic mid-trial resume. Clearing browser storage removes saved results.

The field-report activity uses an actual text area with a 400-character limit. Its completion check verifies the picture sequence and a minimum explanation length; **it does not grade the scientific meaning of the writing**. Saved reports explicitly request teacher review. Download Saved Reports exports the records and explanations as `lumify-science-field-reports.json`.

## Scientific model boundaries

- Iron responds to the magnet; wood, stone, and copper do not. The model does not imply that every metal is magnetic.
- Sieve openings and particle sizes use enlarged illustrative units.
- Paper retains insoluble solids while dissolved salt passes with water. Dry powders cannot be separated by the paper-filter route in Recovery Circuit.
- Evaporation accounts for water leaving as vapor; it does not present the vapor as collected liquid water.
- Decantation is imperfect: steep, unsettled, or excessive pouring carries sediment into the receiver.
- Recovered liquid is not presented as automatically safe to drink. Community recovery units are game quantities, not measured engineering yields.
- These interactive models support instruction and do not replace supervised practical experiments or teacher assessment.
