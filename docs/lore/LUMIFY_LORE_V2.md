# LUMIFY — World and Lore Bible, Version 2.0

> **The learner helps the world remember by learning.**

## How to read this bible

This is the proposed narrative canon for LUMIFY, not a description of features already shipped. **Inherited canon** means an idea retained from the preserved `LUMIFY_LORE_V1_ARCHIVE.md`; **V2 refinement** means a rule or detail adopted here to make that canon coherent; **future reveal** means writer-facing truth that the opening must conceal; **open question** means a choice requiring project-owner confirmation. The current Phaser prototype is called *Lumi Adventures*: it plays five Science games in Vulcan Caverns, while Numeric Ruins and Reading Island are locked. It has no Grand Library scene, Quill, story state, or durable progression. All scenes and effects described below are **planned narrative presentation** unless explicitly identified as existing. The named capstone DOCX was unavailable, so the project's formal scope is taken from the supplied brief and V1 lore, not an invented manuscript reading.

## 1. Core identity

Lumora is a fantasy world shaped by understanding. At its heart stands the **Grand Library**, a living archive through which the Knowledge Realms remain connected. A spreading loss of understanding, called **the Fading**, has weakened those connections. The Library sends an **Invitation** to a young learner beyond its damaged network. The learner who answers becomes a **Luminary**. **Quill — the Young Paper Fox Spellkeeper**, an enchanted origami fox and the Library's youngest Keeper, accompanies them.

A Luminary's tools are observation, curiosity, explanation, revision, and persistence. No enemy needs defeating. The central promise is intimate rather than grandiose: a learner can notice a problem, make sense of it, and leave one place a little clearer than they found it. **No child is responsible for causing the Fading or for restoring all of Lumora alone.**

## 2. Lumora and the laws of understanding

### The Living Weave — V2 refinement

Lumora has ordinary physical processes as well as a **Living Weave**: an ancient network of knowledge-bound places and works. A plant still needs water and light; a stone still falls when dropped. The Weave lets communities coordinate natural systems and maintain extraordinary structures: a terraced garden responds to careful observation, a calibrated bridge can adjust to shifting ground, and a message stone carries meaning between speakers. These works were made by people applying and sharing understanding over generations. The Library keeps a record of the relationships that sustain them.

Three principles govern the Weave:

1. **A record is not understanding.** A book can keep words, diagrams, and observations. Understanding appears when someone can use them, test them against the world, explain them, and revise an error. The Library can preserve traces but cannot turn unread marks into a living practice by itself.
2. **A bond is maintained through use and exchange.** Knowledge-bound places are strongest when their ideas are practiced locally and connected to other fields. A bridge's measurements, materials, maintenance instructions, and shared language all matter. A forgotten formula alone does not make every bridge collapse.
3. **The Weave fails locally before the ordinary world fails.** When a bond thins, an enchanted mechanism stops adjusting, an old waterwork loses its careful rhythm, or a message stone becomes unreadable. Ordinary processes continue, but a dependent structure may become unsafe or an ecosystem shaped by that structure may suffer. This is fantasy world logic, not a scientific account of how real rivers or bridges work.

Thus the collapse of an ancient bridge is credible when its knowledge-bound supports cease correcting a long-standing weakness; a normal bridge that is sound does not vanish because a child forgot geometry. In Vulcan Caverns, a Faded irrigation channel can fail to distribute water, affecting nearby plants. The water cycle itself does not forget how to operate.

### Why subjects matter together — V2 refinement

Each realm keeps a powerful way of asking questions. Science observes and tests; Mathematics measures and models; languages preserve and communicate; social knowledge remembers collective choices; values guide care; practical skills turn ideas into useful work; the arts, movement, and health help people express and sustain lives. A strong local practice can repair a **local bond**. Yet a bridge maintained without communication, or an observation never measured or shared, leaves a gap in the Weave. **True Restoration** requires renewed exchange among realms. This gives single-subject learning real value without pretending one subject alone resolves the larger crisis.

## 3. The Grand Library

**Inherited canon:** The oldest living archive stands at Lumora's center. Its amber pulse and shifting halls show the health of the Weave. It connects the realms and sends the Invitation. Its voice is rare.

**V2 refinement:** The Library stores records and maps relations among them; it does not control natural law, issue perfect answers, or grade students. Dark shelves hold physical volumes whose connections have become hard to interpret. A few pages may appear blank because their living ink has lost its meaning, while the paper and ordinary marks remain. “Empty shelves” in older accounts are an impression of extinguished light, not proof that every book vanished. The Library can keep a fragile local repair from slipping immediately, but it cannot complete a broken understanding for someone else.

The Library speaks only at an Invitation, a genuine first restoration, a substantial mastery milestone, or a later discovery about connections. It does **not** announce every correct answer, supply routine instructions, or shame mistakes. Example, after the first credible milestone: “A thread holds again. Follow where it leads.” Silence remains part of its character.

## 4. The Fading: history, symptoms, and limits

### What characters know at the beginning

The Fading is a name for weakening bonds in the Living Weave. Familiar paths appear less reliable: a demonstration device no longer responds, an old irrigation channel misdirects water, a calibrated arch shifts, a message stone loses readable text. People retain ordinary memories and can still learn; the Fading is neither universal amnesia nor a punishment for forgetting a lesson. Books remain physically present, though some once-living inscriptions dim. The damage spreads unevenly along weakened connections, so two nearby places may look different. It does not expand when a learner makes a wrong answer.

No one knows the first day of the present Fading. Keepers have found old notes about isolated failures, but cannot tell whether those were the same phenomenon. **Vulcan Caverns is where Quill first has a reliable report and a safe route for the learner, not the proven birthplace of the Fading.** The farmer, coastal teacher, and bridge stories in older telling are unverified reports from different places, not a timeline of first occurrence. The Library has existed for many generations; the Invitation is an old response to a broad crisis, but the current Keepers do not know when it was last used, if ever.

### Writer-facing underlying rule — future reveal

Over many generations, the realms grew specialized. Their people kept many local records yet practiced less exchange between fields. The Weave lost the cross-realm confirmations that made its old bonds resilient. **Fragmentation is the underlying condition that allows the Fading to spread**, without a villain or a single disastrous decision. This is not disclosed as settled fact in Act I. Later evidence should make the hypothesis plausible before a future chapter confirms it. This choice does not blame children, teachers, or any one realm.

Local practice can stabilize a damaged site; incomplete learning may create a brief **glimmer** of its former pattern. Repeated, demonstrated understanding can support a steadier repair. A local repair can need continued practice and care; it is neither an instant cure nor something erased by one later mistake. Neither a single correct choice nor the end of one Science chapter permanently cures the Fading everywhere. Lasting True Restoration depends on renewed connections across the Weave. The exact first event that tipped the system from fragility into the present crisis remains an **intentionally unresolved mystery** for later writers, even after the thematic mechanism is known.

## 5. The Invitation, Luminaries, and Keepers

When the Library could no longer reconcile enough weakened paths from within its own archive, it sent the Invitation beyond the Weave. It feels like a question worth following, never a command. A learner who answers receives a way to notice faint relationships in Lumora and to bring an **independent act of understanding** back into the network. This is why Luminaries matter: their learning is not merely an echo of damaged records.

Keepers can study and learn. They preserve material, maintain safe routes, ask questions, and verify observations. But their knowledge is bound to the same compromised archive, and no Keeper can repair the whole Weave by reciting a stored answer. Quill can help recover a local thread alongside the learner; he cannot replace the learner's outside perspective or the many other people whose practices sustain a realm. The Invitation's purpose is partnership, not a prophecy that one child must succeed alone.

**Each student plays an independent instance of Lumora**, like a separate save file in a single-player story game. Every instance has the same setting, history, characters, realms, Invitation, Fading, and underlying rules. Within it, the learner's activities alone determine their discoveries, milestones, progression, and environmental restoration. One learner's repaired Mixture Gallery does not illuminate another learner's Gallery or change their Quill's dialogue. No collective restoration, classroom-wide world state, multiplayer dependency, or shared narrative progression exists. The story itself simply addresses each learner as the Luminary on their own adventure; it need not explain separate saves as alternate timelines or universes. Teacher analytics may aggregate educational performance outside the fiction, but that aggregation never changes another student's story. **Per-student saved restoration is proposed, not implemented** in the audited Phaser prototype.

## 6. Quill and the Library's voice

**Quill — the Young Paper Fox Spellkeeper** is the Grand Library's youngest Keeper and an apprentice **Spellkeeper**, his magical specialization rather than a second official rank. Keepers are enchanted beings formed from materials used to preserve knowledge; Quill was brought to life from folded parchment, living ink, and a trace of the Library's amber light. He first organized lower shelves, maintained small knowledge-bound objects, and studied elementary archival magic. Accompanying the Luminary is his first major journey beyond the Library. He knows its rooms, basic maps, old reports, and a few useful spells, but has little practical experience outside its halls. He cannot diagnose every Faded site, does not know the Fading's cause, and cannot tell whether old incident notes describe the same crisis. He fears arriving too late to preserve a story or help someone who needs it. He goes because a guide must see what the archive misses, and because he wants to become a Keeper who understands more than he can recite.

Quill is curious, clever, friendly, expressive, slightly nervous, and determined. He starts eager and overprepared, believing a great Spellkeeper must remember every answer. He offers a question before an explanation, admits when a guess fails, and notices observations the learner makes before he does. By the end of the first chapter, practice has taught him to say “Let's check” instead of “The book says.” He is more confident investigating unfamiliar ideas, not suddenly all-knowing. His humor comes from earnest moments—“I brought three maps. Two disagree. The third is a recipe”—rather than a repeated failed-spell joke. He never claims that one answer proves mastery, talks down to the learner, or narrates every click.

**Visual identity — planned:** Quill is a small, handmade origami fox with clear triangular ears, a layered folded-paper tail, slight uneven creases, living-ink markings, and warm amber eyes. A short scholar's mantle and one ear-notched, slightly oversized wizard hat give him an apprentice silhouette; a tiny satchel carries notes and a quill stylus. Keep the ink glyphs and amber accents sparse enough to read at small pixel-art sizes. His hat, mantle, and satchel signal study and travel, not combat or authority. Feasible portrait or scripted animations include an ear twitch, tail sway, head tilt, small hop, rustling folds, a brief hat slip, and one or two amber glyphs. A short paper glide can serve a transition; he needs no controllable sprite or free-roaming NPC behavior. No Quill art or animation exists in the audited prototype.

**Minor archival magic — planned narrative ability:** Quill can guide a little living ink to highlight surviving words, fold a page into a map, kindle a small amber light, and trace a faint glyph or relationship for closer inspection. He can consult surviving archive records and briefly glide by opening a paper fold. These are ways to reveal, preserve, and illustrate clues; they do not create missing understanding. His light may expose a damaged diagram, but only observation and reasoning can determine what it means. He cannot restore the Fading automatically, supply correct assessment answers, repair a realm by spell, override the Library's limits, or fight with offensive magic. His wizard identity makes investigation feel magical while keeping the Luminary's learning necessary.

**Judge Wise** already appears in Science Court as a fixed, drawn judge. A light proposed integration makes the court an old Vulcan practice for testing claims against evidence. The judge asks for a classification and reasons, then returns the existing verdict. He need not become a traveling NPC or a keeper of secret lore. Quill may introduce him once: “Judge Wise likes evidence more than confident guesses. I think we'll get along.”

## 7. The eight Knowledge Realms

The first three names and identities are **inherited canon**. The five further realm names and details are **V2 proposed canon for future worldbuilding only**. They have no current levels, questions, assessment data, analytics, or playable routes. Within each student's Lumora, all eight belong to one Weave; their subjects are ways of understanding that world, not isolated territories that teach only one fact.

**Map rule — refined visual canon:** The Grand Library is the visual center of one compact, ocean-grounded eight-realm archipelago. Every coast or sea-level isle meets the same water; none of the map's regions levitates. Fine living threads cross the short channels and show that the realms once exchanged understanding. **Vulcan is open from the beginning** because its thread is the first route the Keepers can still verify as stable and safe for the learner, and its reports offer a clear place to begin. Its terrain is not fully restored. Numeric Ruins and Reading Island are **near, charted primary realms awaiting playable content**; their silhouettes sit in light mist and their threads are interrupted. The five outer realms are **distant, deliberately sealed folios** under deeper, dark magical cloud on this chapter's live chart. Each of the eight must remain visibly present in the proposed map, even where its route cannot be followed. The cloud and mist represent the Library's uncertain view of weakened knowledge paths, not permanent weather over every resident. Keepers may retain archived names, but the live map displays nameplates only for the Library and a currently open realm. It does not show Numeric, Reading, or the five future names by default, in tooltips, or in a player-facing atlas until a later playable route is introduced. This is a presentation rule, not a claim that an inaccessible place has no identity. Science completion must not promise to unlock another realm. The audited prototype still shows only the original three islands with fixed locks and no central Library.

## 10. Restoration and learning progression — design specification, not implemented

Narrative responses should acknowledge learning without equating a click with mastery. The fantasy rule and an eventual educational evidence rule are related but distinct: the story can show a **glimmer** for effort, while stronger persistent restoration requires demonstrated understanding under the project's approved educational criteria.

| Stage | Meaning | Appropriate planned response | Must not imply |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Attempt** | Student engages, including an incorrect or unfinished response. | Quill invites observation; a faint clue becomes easier to see. | Damage, blame, lost world health, or proof of mastery. |
| **Progress** | Student improves, gets part of a sequence right, or revises an explanation. | A local lantern flickers; labels clarify briefly. | Permanent repair from one answer. |
| **Completion** | A mini-game ends or an assessment is finished, regardless of result. | A record of the work appears; Quill recognizes the question pursued. | Automatic mastery or a realm unlock. |
| **Mastery** | Student meets an educator-approved threshold with suitable evidence. The current quiz uses 80% pass, but no cross-system mastery policy is verified. | A site holds a steady light; a Library shelf regains a readable connection. | That the student is now perfect or the whole realm is cured. |
| **Local Restoration** | Supported understanding stabilizes a specific knowledge-bound place. | Mixture Gallery labels and a channel marker remain clear in that student's Lumora instance. | True Restoration across all realms. |
| **True Restoration** | Later, distinct fields reconnect through shared practice. | Wider paths in the Weave become reliable. | An Act I reveal or current-capstone feature. |

**Recommended future state model:** for each student independently, track `introSeen`, lesson attempt/completion, separately recorded assessment outcome, and awarded local story milestones keyed to stable lesson IDs. That student's world displays only their own restored sites and discoveries; another student's records never change it. Treat mini-game win and assessment pass as separate evidence. Do not infer mastery from a single arbitrary answer or award it on a failed assessment merely because the mini-game ended. If a mini-game is lost, the current prototype still permits its FT/ST; the story should call that a chance to test a different kind of understanding, never a forbidden shortcut. A teacher-approved policy would decide which evidence supports a persistent milestone. The prototype has none of this storage, restoration state, or Laravel handoff. Aggregate teacher analytics, if later implemented, remain educational reports outside the fictional world state.

## 11. The central mystery and future reveal

The opening question is **what is happening to the world's shared understanding?** The first chapter answers a smaller question: careful observation and explanation can steady a damaged local bond. It then offers a clue that a scientific observation also needs measurement and language to travel. Quill notices the clue but does not solve it for the learner.

**Future reveal for writers:** The present Fading spreads through weakened cross-realm exchange. The realms were never meant to function as sealed subjects. True Restoration is renewed exchange—not an enemy's defeat, a secret weapon, or a single perfect test score. This is a direction for later chapters, not dialogue to reveal in the introduction. Whether one historic interruption accelerated the recent spread remains open; do not invent a culprit merely to answer it.

## 12. Expansion and educational alignment

The current educational scope named in the brief is **Science, Mathematics, and English**. The audited prototype has playable Science Q1 Mixtures only; Mathematics and English remain locked. The five outer realms are future story geography, not present curricular implementation. Formal manuscript requirements, including exact grade/quarter coverage and analytics obligations, remain unverified because the named DOCX was unavailable. The world bible therefore avoids treating proposed progression, dashboard analytics, or teacher controls as shipped features.

Lumora can support later Science topics, the other two primary subjects, and eventually the five sealed subjects without rewriting its central law: a realm can practice a local understanding, while wider health depends on exchanging understanding. Future content should credit learners' reasoning and revision, not turn assessment scores into moral worth. Any cross-subject quest should be designed with educators and need not become a combat or multiplayer system.

## 13. Narrative style guide

- **Tone:** Warm, curious, lightly mysterious, hopeful. Prefer concrete things a Grade 6 learner can observe over abstract speeches.
- **Dialogue:** One idea per short line. Quill asks and reacts; his spells point to clues instead of reciting a lesson's answer. The Library speaks rarely and in brief, calm sentences.
- **Emotional path:** Curiosity → concern → purpose → earned accomplishment → wonder. Keep concern proportionate; avoid apocalyptic pressure on a child.
- **Mistakes:** A wrong answer reveals a clue or a better question. It never worsens the Fading, costs a character's trust, or erases previous learning.
- **Mystery:** Show symptoms first; let later evidence challenge early guesses. Do not claim the full cause in an opening tooltip.
- **Educational integrity:** Science, mathematics, languages, and other subjects work normally in the real world. Lumora's Weave is explicitly fantasy. Assessments measure defined learning evidence; story effects are metaphors, not scientific claims.
- **Language and inclusion:** Names for Filipino subjects identify learning areas, not a single monolithic culture. Avoid implying that a child who learns slowly is less worthy of the Invitation. Do not require sound, fast reading, or color discrimination to follow a story clue.
